# User Personas — NorthBridge One

> [!NOTE]
> Based on secondary research and simulated stakeholder discovery. No real interviews were conducted. This is a portfolio prototype.

## 1. Dr. James Chen — Principal
**Photo Description**: Professional, mid-50s, wearing a suit, looking thoughtful in an office setting.
**Demographics**: 55, Ed.D., 20+ years in education.
**Role**: Principal. Provides strategic oversight, manages school reputation, and reports to the board.
**Goals**:
1. Ensure high academic standards and student wellbeing.
2. Have clear, real-time visibility into school operations.
3. Reduce staff turnover by minimizing administrative burnout.
4. Manage crises proactively before they escalate.
**Frustrations**:
1. Finding out about serious issues from angry parents rather than staff.
2. Waiting weeks for IT to generate custom reports.
3. Disconnected systems making it hard to see the "big picture."
4. Inability to track if strategic initiatives are actually being executed.
**A Day in the Life**: Starts the day reviewing emails and dealing with escalated issues. Spends most of the day in meetings with staff, parents, or board members. Often relies on end-of-day summaries from Vice-Principals to understand what happened in the school.
**Key Quote**: *"I need to know the health of my school right now, not what it was at the end of last term."*
**Tech Comfort Level**: Moderate. Uses dashboards, but dislikes learning complex new tools.

## 2. Ms. Priya Sharma — Grade 8 Teacher
**Photo Description**: Energetic, early 30s, standing in a classroom holding a tablet.
**Demographics**: 32, M.Ed., 8 years teaching experience.
**Role**: Grade 8 Science Teacher and Homeroom Advisor.
**Goals**:
1. Maximize time spent teaching and interacting with students.
2. Identify struggling students early.
3. Communicate effectively with parents without it taking all evening.
4. Keep track of specific accommodations for diverse learners.
**Frustrations**:
1. Entering the same attendance or grade data into multiple systems.
2. Not knowing if a student is acting out because of issues in other classes.
3. Spending hours compiling notes for parent-teacher conferences.
4. Interventions feeling like a black hole—she reports an issue and never hears back.
**A Day in the Life**: Arrives early to prep. Teaches 5 periods. Uses breaks to chase up missing assignments, log behavior incidents, and reply to parent emails. Often takes administrative work home.
**Key Quote**: *"I want to teach, not be a data entry clerk. Give me the context I need, and let me get back to my students."*
**Tech Comfort Level**: High. Rapidly adopts tools that save her time.

## 3. Sarah Williams — Administrator
**Photo Description**: Organized, late 40s, at a desk with dual monitors and a headset.
**Demographics**: 48, B.A. Business Admin, 15 years in school admin.
**Role**: Operations Administrator. Coordinates across departments, manages records.
**Goals**:
1. Maintain 100% accurate student records.
2. Ensure smooth daily operations (substitutes, event logistics).
3. Resolve staff and parent queries quickly.
4. Automate repetitive communication tasks.
**Frustrations**:
1. Chasing teachers for attendance registers every morning.
2. Reconciling conflicting data between the SIS and other platforms.
3. Being the middle-person for messages that should go directly to teachers.
4. Managing complex workflows with spreadsheets and sticky notes.
**A Day in the Life**: The hub of the school. Constantly fielding phone calls, emails, and walk-ins. Spends significant time manually moving data from one system to another and tracking down people for answers.
**Key Quote**: *"If I have to copy-paste one more list from an email into a spreadsheet, I might scream."*
**Tech Comfort Level**: High. Master of workarounds and spreadsheet formulas.

## 4. David Morgan — Parent
**Photo Description**: Busy professional, early 40s, looking at a smartphone while holding a coffee.
**Demographics**: 42, IT Professional, father of Alex (Grade 8).
**Role**: Parent. Wants to ensure his child is succeeding and safe.
**Goals**:
1. Know how Alex is performing academically and socially.
2. Be alerted immediately if there is a problem.
3. Easily communicate with teachers and staff.
4. Manage school fees and consent forms simply.
**Frustrations**:
1. Having to log into three different portals to see grades, pay fees, and read notices.
2. Finding out Alex has been struggling in Math only at the end-of-term report.
3. Missing important school emails because they get lost in his inbox.
4. Filling out the same medical information forms every year.
**A Day in the Life**: Works full-time. Tries to stay involved in Alex's education but struggles to find the time to navigate complex school portals. Usually only interacts with the school when there's an issue or a fee due.
**Key Quote**: *"Just give me one app where I can see everything about Alex's day. I don't have time to hunt for information."*
**Tech Comfort Level**: Very High. Expects consumer-grade software experiences.

## 5. Michael Torres — Finance Manager
**Photo Description**: Detail-oriented, late 30s, looking at financial spreadsheets.
**Demographics**: 39, CPA, 5 years at the school.
**Role**: Finance Manager. Handles billing, collections, and financial reporting.
**Goals**:
1. Ensure timely fee collection to maintain cash flow.
2. Accurate financial reporting to the Principal and Board.
3. Reduce manual reconciliation errors.
4. Streamline the invoicing process.
**Frustrations**:
1. Disconnect between the student roster and the billing system.
2. Manually following up on overdue accounts.
3. Not knowing if a family is experiencing hardship before sending a harsh collection email.
4. Processing complex pro-rated refunds for mid-year withdrawals.
**A Day in the Life**: Focused on spreadsheets and accounting software. Spends significant time coordinating with Admin to verify student statuses before running billing cycles.
**Key Quote**: *"Finance doesn't operate in a vacuum. I need to know when a student's status changes immediately, not at the end of the month."*
**Tech Comfort Level**: High with financial tools, moderate with general IT.
